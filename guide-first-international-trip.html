Dispatch · First timers

Your first international trip

Everything that has to happen between deciding to go and clearing passport control on the other end, in the order it has to happen.

The morning of, which is the only genuinely difficult part. Photograph via Unsplash.

Meridian Field GuideIssue No. 14 Portland, Oregon · 08/18/2026Page 1

By Hana Whitfield  ·  Published 07/22/2026  ·  Last verified 08/12/2026  ·  13 min read

Roughly half of American adults hold a valid passport, which means roughly half do not, and a fair number of those are one decision away from a first trip abroad. If that is you, the good news is that the process is bureaucratic rather than difficult. The bad news is that the bureaucracy has lead times, and lead times are the only part of this that cannot be fixed with money on the day.

Work backwards from your intended departure. The passport is first, everything else follows, and the whole sequence takes about three months if you are unhurried and about six weeks if you are not.

The passport, and the six-month rule

A first adult passport must be applied for in person at an acceptance facility, which is usually a post office or a county clerk's office, and it requires an appointment at most locations. Routine processing has run in the range of four to six weeks in recent periods, with expedited service several weeks faster for an additional fee. Those windows move, sometimes substantially, so check the State Department's published processing times rather than a figure you read somewhere last year.

The rule that catches people is not the processing time. It is the six-month validity requirement. A large number of countries require that your passport remain valid for at least six months beyond your date of entry, and airlines enforce this at check-in because they are the ones fined if you are refused entry. A passport expiring in four months is, for practical purposes, already expired.

Check the expiration date today, before you read the rest of this. If it is inside a year, renew it now. Renewal by mail is straightforward for adults whose most recent passport was issued within the last fifteen years, after age sixteen, and is undamaged.

Visas, and the things that are not visas

Most of the destinations Americans visit first do not require a visa in advance. Canada, Mexico, the United Kingdom, the Schengen countries, Japan, South Korea and Australia all admit US passport holders for short tourist stays without a traditional visa, though several require an electronic authorization that is not called a visa but functions like one.

The United Kingdom now operates an Electronic Travel Authorisation. The European Union has been implementing an entry-exit system and a linked travel authorization scheme, ETIAS, on a schedule that has moved more than once. Australia requires an ETA. Canada requires an eTA for air arrivals.

Each of these is an online form costing between roughly $10 and $40, approved in most cases within minutes to a few days. The only reliable source for whether you need one, and what it currently costs, is the destination government's own website. Commercial sites that offer to file it for you charge several times the government fee for typing your details into the same form.

Global Entry, TSA PreCheck, or neither

TSA PreCheck costs $78 for five years and speeds you through domestic security. Global Entry costs $120 for five years, includes PreCheck, and speeds you through US customs on the way home. If you will leave the country even once in five years, Global Entry is the better buy for $42 more.

The bottleneck is the interview. Enrollment centers have run appointment backlogs of weeks to months depending on the city, though Global Entry now offers enrollment on arrival at many airports, which lets you complete the interview when you land from an international trip. Apply early, and check whether your credit card reimburses the fee, because a large number of travel cards do.

Neither program is necessary. Both are conveniences. If you are flying once and the trip is six weeks away, skip them and arrive at the airport three hours early instead.

Getting a phone to work abroad

Your US carrier will offer an international day pass, typically around $10 to $12 per day per line. For a ten-day trip with two people that is $200 to $240, which buys a great deal of dinner.

The alternative is an eSIM. A prepaid data eSIM covering most of Europe or most of Asia runs roughly $8 to $20 for 5GB over one to two weeks. Your phone needs to be unlocked and eSIM-capable, which in practice means most models from around 2019 onward. Install the profile before you fly, on hotel or home wifi, and leave your US line active but with data roaming switched off so you still receive calls and two-factor authentication codes.

One caution: some banks send verification codes only by SMS to a US number, and some of those messages do not arrive reliably abroad. Set up an authenticator app before you go rather than discovering this at a hotel desk in Lisbon.

Money

Bring a credit card with no foreign transaction fee, and a second card from a different network kept somewhere other than your wallet. The standard foreign transaction fee is 3 percent, which on a $3,000 trip is $90 for nothing at all.

Take some local currency out of a bank ATM on arrival, not from a currency exchange counter in the terminal, whose rates are consistently poor. When any card machine or ATM offers to charge you in US dollars rather than the local currency, decline. That is dynamic currency conversion and it costs you between 3 and 12 percent.

Tell your bank you are traveling, or better, set the travel notice in the app. Fraud systems are much better than they were, but a first foreign transaction on a card that has never left the country still triggers holds.

Insurance, honestly

Most US health plans provide little or no coverage outside the country, and Medicare provides essentially none. That is the actual reason to buy travel insurance, and it is a good one. A medical evacuation from a remote area can run into six figures.

What you want is a policy with a medical minimum around $100,000 and an evacuation minimum around $250,000. Trip cancellation coverage is a separate and much more expensive component, and it is genuinely optional; buy it if the trip is expensive and non-refundable, skip it if you are staying in hotels with free cancellation.

The four documents to photograph

Before you leave the house, photograph the picture page of your passport, your travel insurance card with the emergency assistance number, the front and back of the credit cards you are carrying, and your printed itinerary. Store them somewhere that does not require your phone to be working, which means email them to yourself as well as saving them locally.

Then write the US embassy's phone number for your destination on a piece of paper and put it in the bag you are not carrying. This sounds excessive until the one time it is not.

The landing card, and the questions

Some countries still hand out a paper arrival card on the plane. Fill it in before you land, in block capitals, using the address of your first night's accommodation, which is the question that reliably catches people who booked three different hotels.

At passport control you will be asked the purpose of your visit, how long you are staying and where. Answer plainly. Tourism, nine days, and the name of the hotel. There is no advantage to elaboration.

The sequence, in order

12 weeks out
Apply for or renew the passport. Check the six-month validity rule against your return date.
10 weeks out
Book flights and the first two nights of lodging. Apply for Global Entry if you want it.
6 weeks out
File any electronic travel authorization the destination requires. Buy travel insurance.
2 weeks out
Set the bank travel notice. Buy and install the eSIM profile. Set up an authenticator app.
The night before
Photograph the four documents. Fill in the landing card if you have one.
The bureaucracy is not difficult. It is just slow, and slowness is the one thing money cannot fix on the day. Hana Whitfield, contributing editor
