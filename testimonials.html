How these were collected

Fourteen days after a trip's end date, we email everyone who used the planner for it and ask two questions: what worked, and what did not. Roughly one in nine replies. We publish a review only with written permission, and we publish it as written, correcting spelling and nothing else.

Nobody is paid, entered into a prize draw or given a discount for a review. We do not remove a review because it is critical, and several below are. Where a reviewer asked us to withhold a surname or a city, we have.

What readers complain about, and what we are doing

Aggregated from all 2,417 replies, not just the twelve above.

The destination index is too short

How often
Named in 31 percent of replies. Comfortably the most common criticism.
Our answer
It is short because we send a writer to every place we cover and pay for the trip. We are adding four destinations a year, and the next two are Croatia and the Oregon coast.

No booking integration

How often
Named in 18 percent of replies.
Our answer
We are not going to build it. Booking integration turns a publication into a retailer, and the incentive to recommend whatever pays best becomes irresistible. This is a deliberate limitation.

Costs run slightly over

How often
Named in 12 percent of replies, with a median overrun around 9 percent.
Our answer
That is close to the 12 percent contingency we recommend in the budget estimator, and we have started surfacing it in the planner output too.

Collaborator limits on the free tier

How often
Named in 7 percent of replies, almost entirely from people planning group trips.
Our answer
We added a $3 per trip single-seat add-on rather than forcing a subscription. It is on the pricing page.
