Sample Itinerary · Utah

Five parks, seven days,
912 miles

Las Vegas to Salt Lake City with Scenic Byway 12 given the whole day it deserves, and every permit deadline flagged before you book.

Late light on the Colorado Plateau, third week of April. Photograph via Unsplash.

Meridian Field GuideIssue No. 14 Portland, Oregon · 08/18/2026Page 1

By Curtis Boyle  ·  Published 02/17/2026  ·  Last verified 08/11/2026

The brief this was built from

Two travelers, seven days on the ground, flying into Las Vegas on 04/18/2026 and out of Salt Lake City on 04/25/2026. Balanced pace, moderate budget tier, interests set to outdoors and history. A midsize SUV collected at LAS and dropped at SLC.

One-way is the right call here even with the drop fee. The alternative is 234 miles of I-15 retracing on the last day, which costs you five hours and a park.

Book these before anything else

  • Angels Landing permit. Quarterly advance lottery, plus a day-before lottery. This itinerary does not include Angels Landing, but if you want it, the lottery is the first calendar entry you make.
  • Arches timed entry. Required in the spring peak window. Released three months ahead, with next-day tickets at 7 p.m. Mountain Time.
  • Springdale and Moab lodging. Both sell out first and price hardest. Book these before the flights.

The seven days

Every cost line covers both travelers together: one room, two sets of meals, park fees and the day's fuel. The rental car is billed separately at $78 per day.

Day 1  ·  04/18/2026

Las Vegas to Springdale  ·  162 miles

Arrival, groceries, and one short trail

Land mid-morning, buy the cooler in St. George rather than Springdale, and be at a trailhead for the last two hours of light.

  • 10:30 a.m.

    Collect the car, then the supermarket in St. George

    Cooler, four gallons of water, breakfast items and trail food. The same basket in Springdale costs about 40 percent more.

    1 hr 50 min drive · allow 60 min · $118 for two

  • 2:30 p.m.

    Check in at Springdale, then the Zion visitor center

    Park at the hotel and take the free town shuttle. Buy the $80 America the Beautiful annual pass here; it pays for itself at the third park.

    50 min drive · allow 75 min · $80 annual pass

  • 5:00 p.m.

    Canyon Overlook Trail

    One mile round trip through the Zion-Mount Carmel tunnel section, and the best view-to-effort ratio in the park. Parking is roughly a dozen spaces, which is the only difficulty.

    20 min drive up the switchbacks · allow 75 min · covered by the pass

Estimated day 1 cost $444

Lodging $178 · groceries and meals $152 · annual pass $80 · fuel $34.

Day 2  ·  04/19/2026

Zion Canyon, all day  ·  7 miles on foot

Up the canyon on the first shuttle

Private vehicles cannot drive above Canyon Junction in season, which is the reason Zion still functions. The first shuttle is the difference between a good day and a queue.

  • 6:15 a.m.

    Riverside Walk and the Narrows gateway

    A paved mile to where the canyon walls close in, then wade upstream as far as flow and footwear allow. Rent neoprene socks and canyon boots in town if the water is under 55°F.

    40 min shuttle to the last stop · allow 240 min · $64 gear rental for two

  • 11:30 a.m.

    Zion Lodge lawn, lunch from the cooler

    Shade, restrooms and the biggest cottonwoods you will see all week. The only food service inside the canyon.

    25 min shuttle · allow 60 min · from the cooler

  • 1:00 p.m.

    Emerald Pools, lower and middle

    Two miles round trip, shaded for most of it, ending at a hanging garden with water dripping off the overhang.

    5 min walk from the lodge · allow 105 min · covered by the pass

  • 4:30 p.m.

    Pa'rus Trail at golden hour

    Paved, flat, wheelchair and bicycle accessible, running along the Virgin River. The Watchman turns red about forty minutes before sunset.

    20 min shuttle · allow 90 min · covered by the pass

Estimated day 2 cost $408

Lodging $178 · meals $152 · Narrows gear $64 · fuel $14. No driving beyond the shuttle lot.

Day 3  ·  04/20/2026

Springdale to Bryce Canyon  ·  86 miles

From the canyon floor to the rim

Bryce sits at 8,000 feet and runs roughly 20°F colder than Zion. Pack the fleece even in April, particularly for the evening.

  • 7:45 a.m.

    Zion-Mount Carmel Highway

    Switchbacks, a mile-long 1930 tunnel with windows cut into the rock, and the checkerboard mesa beyond. Oversized vehicles need a tunnel escort, which costs $15 and a wait.

    1 hr 50 min drive · allow 120 min with stops · fuel $28

  • 10:30 a.m.

    Bryce Point and Inspiration Point

    See the shape of the amphitheatre from above before you descend into it. The rim path between the two viewpoints is level and paved.

    15 min drive from the gate · allow 90 min · covered by the pass

  • 12:45 p.m.

    Navajo Loop and Queens Garden

    Three miles, counterclockwise: descend the steep Wall Street switchbacks, climb out the gentle side. Wall Street closes in winter and after heavy rain.

    5 min drive to Sunset Point · allow 180 min · covered by the pass

  • 8:30 p.m.

    Ranger astronomy programme

    Bryce is a certified Dark Sky park and among the darkest in the lower 48. Programmes run several nights a week. Bring a red-filter light and that fleece.

    10 min drive · allow 120 min · free, first come first served

Estimated day 3 cost $358

Lodging $178 · meals $152 · fuel $28. Park entry covered by the annual pass.

Day 4  ·  04/21/2026

Bryce to Torrey on Scenic Byway 12  ·  111 miles

The best drive in the American West, given a whole day

The map says two hours and twenty minutes. Give it eight. This road is the reason to do the loop in this direction.

  • 8:30 a.m.

    Kodachrome Basin State Park

    Sixty-seven sandstone chimneys standing in a basin, on a short detour south of Byway 12. The Angel's Palace trail is 1.5 easy miles for the overview.

    40 min drive · allow 120 min · $20 state park fee, not covered by the federal pass

  • 11:30 a.m.

    Head of the Rocks overlook and the Hogback

    The overlook shows you the road you are about to drive. The Hogback is a ridge section with a drop on both sides and no guardrail. Not the place to pass anyone.

    1 hr 10 min drive · allow 60 min · free

  • 1:15 p.m.

    Lower Calf Creek Falls

    Six miles round trip on sand to a 126-foot waterfall in a hanging canyon. Start before the heat, carry three litres each, and expect the sand to slow you down.

    10 min drive · allow 240 min · $5 day use

  • 6:00 p.m.

    Boulder Mountain to Torrey

    The road climbs to 9,600 feet through aspen and pine, which is a genuinely startling transition after four days of red rock. Watch for deer at dusk.

    1 hr drive · allow 75 min · fuel $32

Estimated day 4 cost $387

Lodging $178 · meals $152 · state park and day use $25 · fuel $32.

Day 5  ·  04/22/2026

Capitol Reef to Moab  ·  148 miles

An orchard, a pie, and a long empty road

Capitol Reef is the least visited of the five and the most surprising. A Mormon settlement with fruit orchards sits inside a hundred-mile wrinkle in the earth's crust.

  • 8:00 a.m.

    Capitol Gorge and the Pioneer Register

    A flat two-mile walk up a wash past names carved into the wall by nineteenth-century travelers, and Fremont petroglyphs further along the scenic drive.

    15 min drive · allow 150 min · covered by the pass

  • 11:00 a.m.

    Fruita orchards and the Gifford Homestead

    Pick fruit in season from orchards planted in the 1880s, then buy a pie from the homestead. They sell out most days before noon.

    10 min drive · allow 90 min · $28 for two, pie included

  • 1:30 p.m.

    Highway 24 east to Hanksville

    Forty-seven miles with nothing in between. Fill the tank in Torrey. This is the emptiest stretch of the entire loop and cell service disappears entirely.

    55 min drive · allow 75 min · fuel $38

  • 4:30 p.m.

    Arrive Moab, State Route 128 the long way

    Turn off I-70 onto SR-128 and follow the Colorado River for 44 miles of red canyon. Thirty minutes longer than the interstate and worth every one.

    1 hr 40 min drive · allow 120 min · free

Estimated day 5 cost $396

Lodging $178 in Moab · meals $152 · homestead $28 · fuel $38.

Day 6  ·  04/23/2026

Arches and Dead Horse Point  ·  62 miles

Through the gate before eight

Arches uses timed entry in the spring peak window and the lots fill regardless. Your reservation is for the gate, not for a parking space, so early still matters.

  • 7:00 a.m.

    Devils Garden: Landscape Arch and Double O

    Landscape Arch is a flat 1.6 miles round trip. Continuing to Double O adds a slickrock fin traverse with real exposure and doubles the distance.

    35 min drive from Moab · allow 210 min · $2 timed entry plus the pass

  • 11:15 a.m.

    Windows Section and Double Arch

    The highest density of arches in the park with the shortest walks. Good in heat because you are never more than ten minutes from the car.

    20 min drive · allow 105 min · covered

  • 2:00 p.m.

    Moab: lunch, laundry and a rest

    The correct use of the hottest part of the desert day. The laundromat on Main Street takes ninety minutes and quarters.

    30 min drive · allow 150 min · $64 for two plus $6 laundry

  • 6:00 p.m.

    Dead Horse Point at sunset

    A gooseneck of the Colorado 2,000 feet below, and one of the great overlooks in the West. A state park with its own fee, separate from the federal pass.

    45 min drive · allow 120 min · $20 state park entry

Estimated day 6 cost $414

Lodging $178 · meals $152 · timed entry $2 · state park $20 · laundry $6 · fuel $56.

Day 7  ·  04/24/2026

Canyonlands to Salt Lake City  ·  281 miles

Sunrise at Mesa Arch, then north

One early start, one great photograph, and then a long straightforward drive with the whole afternoon to spare before an evening flight.

  • 5:45 a.m.

    Mesa Arch at sunrise

    The underside of the arch glows orange for about eight minutes as the sun clears the horizon. Expect thirty photographers on the rock. Arrive forty minutes early.

    45 min drive from Moab · allow 120 min · covered by the pass

  • 8:30 a.m.

    Grand View Point

    A two-mile rim walk to the end of the Island in the Sky mesa, looking 2,000 feet down into two river canyons. The clearest view of the whole plateau system.

    20 min drive · allow 120 min · covered

  • 11:30 a.m.

    Green River Overlook, then the road north

    Ten minutes for the overlook, then US-191 to I-70 and I-15. Fill up in Green River; the next reliable stop is 105 miles on.

    3 hr 45 min drive to SLC · allow 300 min · fuel $62

  • 5:00 p.m.

    Return the car at Salt Lake City

    Allow ninety minutes before a domestic departure, and remember to empty the cooler. The one-way drop fee was paid at pickup.

    15 min from the interstate · allow 60 min · drop fee $185 already paid

Estimated day 7 cost $214

Meals $152 · fuel $62. Departure day, no lodging. Park entry covered by the annual pass.

How to adapt this

If you only have five days

Cut Capitol Reef and Canyonlands. Do Zion for three days, Bryce for one, and drive back to Las Vegas. Skipping two parks is a better trip than driving 912 miles in five days.

If you have ten days

Add two nights in Escalante for the slot canyons, and one at the Grand Canyon North Rim, which is only open mid-May to mid-October and is a completely different experience from the South Rim.

If you are traveling in July or August

Keep the same route and change the hours. Everything on foot happens before 10 a.m. or after 6 p.m. Move the Calf Creek hike on day four to a 7 a.m. start, and treat the middle of the day as driving time. Watch the flash flood forecast every single morning.

If you are traveling with children

Swap Devils Garden on day six for the Windows Section plus Sand Dune Arch, which children can climb on. Add Goblin Valley State Park between Torrey and Moab; it is a basin of mushroom-shaped hoodoos where scrambling is actively permitted, and it is the single most popular stop we have ever recommended to families.
