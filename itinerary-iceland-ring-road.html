Sample Itinerary · Iceland

The Ring Road,
counterclockwise, in seven days

828 miles, four bases, and the south coast handled on days two and three while you are still fresh enough to enjoy it.

The rim path above Fjadrargljufur, late September. Photograph via Unsplash.

Meridian Field GuideIssue No. 14 Portland, Oregon · 08/18/2026Page 1

By Tove Lindqvist  ·  Published 04/08/2026  ·  Last verified 08/06/2026

The brief this was built from

Two travelers, seven days on the ground, departing Boston on 09/26/2026 and returning 10/03/2026. Balanced pace, moderate budget tier, interests set to outdoors and food. A compact SUV with gravel and sand protection, collected at Keflavik.

The direction matters. Almost everyone drives the ring clockwise because the south coast is closest to the airport and the guidebooks are written that way. Going counterclockwise means you still do the south coast early, on days two and three, but you meet the tour buses head-on rather than sitting behind them, and you reach the east fjords on a day when they are empty.

The seven days

Every cost line below covers both travelers together: one room, two sets of meals, all admissions and the day's fuel. The rental car is billed separately at $95 per day.

Day 1  ·  09/26/2026

Keflavik to Reykjavik  ·  42 miles

Landing, groceries, and one long soak

The overnight flight from Boston lands around 6:30 a.m., which gives you a full day you did not expect and a body that disagrees. Keep it light and horizontal.

  • 7:30 a.m.

    Collect the car, then breakfast at the airport bakery

    Take the photos of every existing scratch before you leave the lot. Sand and gravel protection is worth the $18 a day on this route.

    On site · allow 75 min · $24 for two

  • 9:30 a.m.

    Bonus supermarket, Reykjanesbaer

    Buy the cooler bag first, then fill it. Skyr, rye bread, smoked lamb, apples, coffee and four litres of water. This one stop saves roughly $340 across the week.

    10 min drive · allow 60 min · $86 for two

  • 12:00 p.m.

    Check in, Reykjavik 101, then sleep for two hours

    The single best decision of the whole week. Set an alarm. Do not sleep past three or the night is lost.

    35 min drive · allow 180 min · included in lodging

  • 5:00 p.m.

    Sky Lagoon and dinner in Grandi

    Ocean-edge geothermal bathing with the seven-step ritual, then plaice and langoustine at the harbour sheds. Book the lagoon slot before you fly.

    20 min drive · allow 240 min · $156 lagoon plus $92 dinner

Estimated day 1 cost $588

Lodging $205 · food and groceries $202 · lagoon $156 · fuel $25.

Day 2  ·  09/27/2026

Reykjavik to Vik  ·  128 miles

Three waterfalls and the black beach

The densest scenery in the country, all of it within five minutes of the road. Leave before the Golden Circle buses and you own the morning.

  • 7:45 a.m.

    Seljalandsfoss

    The waterfall you walk behind. You will be soaked from the knees down inside four minutes. Wear the shell, seal the phone.

    1 hr 50 min drive · allow 60 min · $6 parking

  • 10:00 a.m.

    Skogafoss and the clifftop stairway

    A 200-foot curtain you can stand directly beneath, plus 527 steps to the platform above and the start of the Fimmvorduhals trail.

    30 min drive · allow 90 min · $7 parking

  • 12:15 p.m.

    Solheimajokull glacier walk

    Guided crampon walk on the outlet glacier. Helmets, harnesses and axes supplied. Book a week ahead in shoulder season.

    25 min drive · allow 210 min · $250 for two

  • 4:30 p.m.

    Reynisfjara black sand beach

    Basalt columns, a sea cave and genuinely lethal sneaker waves. Read the warning light at the entrance and stay above the high water mark.

    30 min drive · allow 75 min · $8 parking

Estimated day 2 cost $687

Lodging $205 · meals $176 · glacier walk $250 · parking $21 · fuel $35.

Day 3  ·  09/28/2026

Vik to Hofn  ·  169 miles

Canyon, glacier lagoon, diamond beach

The best single driving day in Iceland. Everything today is on Route 1 and everything is worth the stop.

  • 8:15 a.m.

    Fjadrargljufur canyon

    A 300-foot mossy gorge with a rim path. Closed for several weeks each spring while the moss recovers, so check road.is the night before.

    50 min drive · allow 90 min · free

  • 10:45 a.m.

    Skaftafell and the Svartifoss walk

    Basalt column waterfall, 1.8 miles round trip on a good path with 450 feet of climb. The visitor center has the only reliable coffee for sixty miles.

    1 hr 10 min drive · allow 150 min · $10 parking

  • 2:15 p.m.

    Jokulsarlon glacier lagoon

    Icebergs calved off Breidamerkurjokull drifting toward the sea, with seals working the channel. The amphibious boat runs to mid-October.

    50 min drive · allow 120 min · $132 for two on the boat

  • 4:45 p.m.

    Diamond Beach, then on to Hofn

    Ice from the lagoon washed back onto black sand across the road. Stay off the larger blocks; they roll without warning.

    2 min drive, then 55 min to Hofn · allow 60 min · free

Estimated day 3 cost $569

Lodging $205 · meals $176 (langoustine in Hofn is worth it) · boat $132 · parking $10 · fuel $46.

Day 4  ·  09/29/2026

Hofn to Egilsstadir and Seydisfjordur  ·  156 miles

The east fjords, which almost nobody drives

The quietest day of the week and, for several of our editors, the favourite one. Long empty coast road, reindeer on the hillsides, and one very good small town at the end.

  • 8:30 a.m.

    Stokksnes and Vestrahorn

    Black sand dunes below a serrated peak, on private land with a small toll. The best photograph in Iceland and about six miles from your bed.

    15 min drive · allow 105 min · $18 for two

  • 11:00 a.m.

    Djupivogur and the coast road

    A fishing village of about 450 people with a working harbour and a good bakery. The road north from here hugs three fjords in ninety minutes.

    1 hr 15 min drive · allow 75 min · $32 for two

  • 2:30 p.m.

    Seydisfjordur over the mountain pass

    A blue church at the end of a rainbow-painted street, a fjord, and Route 93 climbing to 2,000 feet to get there. Check the pass for snow after mid-October.

    1 hr 20 min drive via Egilsstadir · allow 150 min · $28 for two

  • 9:00 p.m.

    Aurora watch from the fjord road

    Drive ten minutes out of town, kill the headlights, and give it forty minutes. Check the aurora forecast on vedur.is before committing to the cold.

    10 min drive · allow 90 min · free

Estimated day 4 cost $507

Lodging $205 · meals $176 · Stokksnes toll $18 · fuel $48 · incidentals $60.

Day 5  ·  09/30/2026

Egilsstadir to Myvatn  ·  106 miles

Into the volcanic north

The landscape changes completely today. Fjords give way to a high desert plateau, then a lake basin that looks like nowhere else on the island.

  • 8:45 a.m.

    Dettifoss, west bank

    The most powerful waterfall in Europe by volume, and loud enough that you feel it in your chest. Road 862 to the west bank is paved; the east bank is not.

    1 hr 40 min drive · allow 105 min · free

  • 11:30 a.m.

    Namafjall Hverir geothermal field

    Boiling mud pots, sulphur vents and ochre ground with no vegetation at all. It smells exactly as bad as it looks. Stay on the marked paths.

    45 min drive · allow 75 min · free

  • 1:30 p.m.

    Dimmuborgir lava formations

    A collapsed lava lake that left standing pillars and arches. The Church Circle loop is a flat mile through the best of it.

    20 min drive · allow 105 min · free

  • 4:00 p.m.

    Myvatn Nature Baths

    The north's answer to the Blue Lagoon at roughly half the price and a fraction of the crowd, overlooking the lake basin.

    15 min drive · allow 150 min · $92 for two

Estimated day 5 cost $505

Lodging $205 · meals $176 · baths $92 · fuel $32.

Day 6  ·  10/01/2026

Myvatn to Borgarnes  ·  268 miles

The long day, broken by a whale and a waterfall

The biggest driving day of the week, and the reason it works is that the two stops are genuinely worth the mileage between them.

  • 8:00 a.m.

    Godafoss

    A 40-foot horseshoe on the way out of the Myvatn basin, twenty minutes from breakfast, and free to walk both banks.

    35 min drive · allow 60 min · free

  • 10:00 a.m.

    Akureyri, coffee and the botanical garden

    Iceland's second city, population 19,000. The northernmost botanical garden in the world, and the best coffee between Reykjavik and the Arctic Circle.

    45 min drive · allow 90 min · $34 for two

  • 12:30 p.m.

    Hvitserkur sea stack, Vatnsnes peninsula

    A 50-foot basalt stack in the shape of a drinking animal, with a seal colony on the beach below. A 20-mile detour off Route 1 and worth it.

    2 hr 15 min drive · allow 90 min · free

  • 5:00 p.m.

    Borgarnes and the Settlement Center

    Two exhibitions covering the ninth-century settlement and Egil's Saga, with an audio guide in English. A good indoor end to a long driving day.

    2 hr 5 min drive · allow 105 min · $44 for two

Estimated day 6 cost $536

Lodging $205 · meals $176 · admissions $78 · fuel $77. The longest fuel line of the week.

Day 7  ·  10/02/2026

Borgarnes to Keflavik, the long way  ·  159 miles

The Golden Circle, run backwards, on the way to the plane

Everything today is a buffer. If the week has run late or the weather has cost you a stop, this is the day you cut. If it has gone well, it is the best possible ending.

  • 8:30 a.m.

    Gullfoss

    Two tiers dropping into a canyon. Arriving before ten puts you ahead of the day tours out of Reykjavik by roughly ninety minutes.

    1 hr 30 min drive · allow 60 min · free

  • 10:15 a.m.

    Geysir geothermal field

    Strokkur erupts every six to ten minutes. Stand upwind. The Great Geysir itself has been largely dormant since 2016.

    10 min drive · allow 60 min · free

  • 12:15 p.m.

    Thingvellir National Park

    Walk the Almannagja gorge between the tectonic plates, and stand where the parliament was founded in 930.

    50 min drive · allow 120 min · $6 parking

  • 3:30 p.m.

    Return the car at Keflavik

    Refuel at the N1 two miles from the terminal, which is cheaper than the airport pump. Allow three hours before an international departure.

    1 hr 25 min drive · allow 60 min · final fuel $62

Estimated day 7 cost $420

Meals $176 · parking $6 · fuel $62 · final night lodging not included, departure is same day.

How to adapt this

If you only have five days

Do not attempt the ring. Drive the south coast to Jokulsarlon and back, staying two nights in Vik and one in Hofn. You lose the east fjords and the north, and you gain about four hours of daylight a day that were previously spent driving.

If you have ten days

Add the Snaefellsnes peninsula after Borgarnes, which is two days, and give Myvatn a second night. Snaefellsnes is frequently described as Iceland in miniature and it is one of the few pieces of marketing language we agree with.

If you are traveling in winter

The ring is possible from November to March but it is a different trip. Daylight drops to five hours, the east fjord passes close without warning, and single-day drives over 150 miles become unwise. Base yourself on the south coast, add an ice cave, and accept that you will see a third as much and remember it twice as clearly.

If you are traveling with children

Cut day six in half by staying a second night in Akureyri. A 268-mile day is a great deal to ask of anybody under twelve, and the Akureyri swimming pool has three slides.
