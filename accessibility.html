Accessibility Statement

What we have built to WCAG 2.1 Level AA, what we know is not there yet, and how to tell us when something blocks you.

Effective 08/01/2026  ·  Last reviewed 08/01/2026

Meridian Field GuideIssue No. 14 Portland, Oregon · 08/18/2026Page 1

1. Our commitment

Meridian Field Guide is committed to making this website usable by everyone, including people who use screen readers, magnification, voice control, switch devices or a keyboard alone. We treat accessibility as a functional requirement rather than a compliance exercise, which is to say a barrier is a bug and gets a bug's priority.

Our target is conformance with the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines version 2.1 at Level AA. We believe the site currently meets that standard in substantially all respects, with the specific exceptions listed in section 4.

2. What has been implemented

Structure and navigation

Landmarks
Every page uses semantic HTML5 landmarks: header, nav, main and footer, with the primary navigation labelled.
Skip link
A skip-to-content link is the first focusable element on every page and becomes visible on focus.
Headings
One h1 per page, with a logical heading order beneath it and no levels skipped for visual effect.
Page titles
Unique and descriptive, naming the page before the site.
Language
Declared as en-US on the html element.

Keyboard and focus

Full keyboard operation
Every interactive control, including the mobile menu, the FAQ accordion, the cookie banner, the planner and the checklist, is reachable and operable by keyboard.
Visible focus
A two-pixel oxblood focus ring with an offset appears on every focusable element. We have not removed default focus styling anywhere.
Escape key
Closes the mobile navigation and returns focus to the control that opened it.
Target size
Interactive controls are at least 44 by 44 pixels with adequate spacing.

Content and media

Alt text
Every image carries descriptive alternative text describing what is in the photograph, not a keyword list. Decorative treatments use CSS rather than images.
Contrast
Body text is a minimum 4.5:1 against its background and large text a minimum 3:1. Text over photographs sits on a gradient scrim engineered to hold that ratio.
Tables
Data tables use caption, thead, scope attributes on header cells, and scroll horizontally inside their own container rather than forcing the page to scroll.
Link text
Descriptive. No bare "click here" or "read more" without context.

Forms

Labels
Every field has a persistent visible label above the input. We do not use placeholder text as a label.
Errors
Inline, next to the field, in words rather than colour alone, announced with role="alert" and linked by aria-describedby.
Required fields
Marked in the label and enforced with the required attribute.

Motion

Reduced motion
All animation, including scroll reveals and the hero parallax, is disabled when the operating system reports a preference for reduced motion.
No autoplay
There is no auto-playing audio or video anywhere on this site, and no carousel that advances on its own.
Content is visible by default
Scroll reveals enhance already-visible content. Nothing depends on an animation completing in order to be readable.

4. Known gaps

Listing these is more useful than claiming full conformance. Each carries a target date and a named owner internally.

Complex data tables on small screens

Issue
The five-column plan comparison table requires horizontal scrolling below roughly 700 pixels. It is reachable and readable, but the interaction is awkward with a screen magnifier.
Plan
A stacked definition-list presentation below 700 pixels. Target: Q4 2026.

Planner output focus management

Issue
When a stop is removed, focus moves to the top of the regenerated day rather than to the next stop in sequence. Nothing is lost, but the position is less predictable than it should be.
Plan
Restore focus to the adjacent stop. Target: Q4 2026.

Photograph descriptions

Issue
Alt text describes each photograph, but a small number of landscape images would benefit from a longer description of the geography being shown.
Plan
Extended descriptions in the caption text, which is available to everyone rather than only to screen reader users. Ongoing.

Third-party advertising

Issue
Where advertising is served by a third party, we do not control the markup inside the ad frame and cannot guarantee its accessibility.
Plan
Ad slots are labelled, sit outside the main reading flow, and never occupy the main content area of the fold. Rejecting advertising cookies removes personalized ads, and Pro removes advertising entirely.

5. Accessibility of the travel information itself

An accessible website is not the same as accessible travel. Several destination files describe places with significant physical barriers, and where we know about them we say so in the text rather than leaving you to discover it at the top of a staircase.

If you are planning a trip with mobility, sensory or cognitive access requirements and the guides do not answer your question, write to support@meridianfieldguide.com. An editor will look at your specific plan and reply with what we actually know, including where we do not know. There is no charge for this.

6. Reporting a barrier

If something on this site blocks you, please tell us. It is the fastest way for us to find out and we treat these reports as bugs, not feedback.

What helps us. The page address, what you were trying to do, the assistive technology and browser you were using, and what happened instead. Any one of those is better than none.

7. Formal approach and enforcement

This statement was prepared on 08/01/2026 based on internal review against the WCAG 2.1 Level AA success criteria. It has not been independently certified by a third-party auditor. We review it at least annually and after any significant change to the site.

If you are not satisfied with our response to a barrier report, you may escalate to hello@meridianfieldguide.com, which reaches the founding editor directly.
