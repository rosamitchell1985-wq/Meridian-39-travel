Sample Itinerary · Japan

Eight days in Kansai,
entirely by train

Six nights in Kyoto, two in Osaka, and not a single hour spent looking for parking. Built around early mornings and late evenings.

Blossom season lighting on a city canal, first week of April. Photograph via Unsplash.

Meridian Field GuideIssue No. 14 Portland, Oregon · 08/18/2026Page 1

By Hana Whitfield  ·  Published 03/02/2026  ·  Last verified 07/30/2026

The brief this was built from

Two travelers, eight days on the ground, departing San Francisco on 05/22/2026 and returning 05/31/2026. Balanced pace, moderate budget tier, interests set to history, food and art. No rental car, no Japan Rail Pass, and an IC card loaded with 5,000 yen each.

The structure is deliberately lopsided: six nights in one Kyoto hotel and two in Osaka at the end. Moving hotels costs roughly ninety minutes each time, and Kyoto is close enough to Nara, Osaka and Uji that day trips are simply cheaper than relocation. The two Osaka nights exist because Osaka at night is a genuinely different city and you cannot see it properly on the last train back.

Late May was chosen over blossom season on purpose. You lose the flowers. You gain 76°F days, gardens at full green, and room rates about 45 percent below the November maple peak.

The eight days

Every cost line covers both travelers together: one room, two sets of meals, all admissions and the day's transport.

Day 1  ·  05/22/2026

Kansai airport to Kyoto  ·  2.1 miles on foot

Arrival, and one temple before you sleep

The San Francisco flight lands mid-afternoon. Do not attempt anything ambitious. One walk and an early dinner will fix the time zone faster than a nap will.

  • 3:40 p.m.

    Haruka limited express to Kyoto Station

    Reserve seats at the JR counter in the arrivals hall. Buy the IC cards at the same time and load 5,000 yen each.

    80 min from KIX · allow 120 min including immigration · $44 for two

  • 6:00 p.m.

    Check in, Kawaramachi

    Central, walkable to Nishiki and one bridge from Gion. Drop bags, change shoes, go straight back out.

    15 min subway · allow 45 min · included in lodging

  • 7:15 p.m.

    Kamo river walk and a first izakaya

    The riverbank between Sanjo and Shijo fills with students and office workers at dusk. Eat where there is a queue of locals rather than a menu in English.

    10 min walk · allow 150 min · $76 for two

Estimated day 1 cost $302

Lodging $168 · meals $90 · airport train $44. Arrival day, so no admissions.

Day 2  ·  05/23/2026

Southern Higashiyama  ·  5.2 miles on foot

Gates at dawn, market at midday, lanes at dusk

Jet lag has you awake at five. Use it. This is the only day of the trip where the alarm is a gift rather than a punishment.

  • 6:30 a.m.

    Fushimi Inari Taisha

    Walk the first thousand gates before the tour groups. Turn at the Yotsutsuji junction unless you want the full summit loop, which is a real hill.

    6 min on the JR Nara Line · allow 120 min · free, open 24 hours

  • 9:35 a.m.

    Breakfast at a Kyoto Station counter

    Grilled fish, rice, miso, pickles. The second-floor concourse counters open at seven and turn over quickly.

    6 min back on the Nara Line · allow 45 min · $22 for two

  • 11:00 a.m.

    Nishiki Market

    Five blocks of pickles, tamagoyaki, knives and yuba. Eat where you buy it. Several stalls now post signs asking you not to walk and eat.

    15 min on the Karasuma line · allow 90 min · $48 for two

  • 2:05 p.m.

    Kiyomizu-dera and the walk down to Gion

    The wooden stage over the hillside, then downhill through Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka. Photography is banned on some private alleys and the fines are enforced.

    20 min on city bus 100 · allow 180 min · $8 for two

Estimated day 2 cost $396

Lodging $168 · meals $144 · transport $16 · admissions $8 · incidentals $60.

Day 3  ·  05/24/2026

Arashiyama and the northwest  ·  4.1 miles on foot

Bamboo before eight, gold in the afternoon

The bamboo grove is roughly 500 yards long and photographs badly once it fills. The first train buys you forty minutes of quiet.

  • 7:15 a.m.

    Arashiyama bamboo grove

    Walk it slowly, then again in the other direction. That is the whole of it, and it is enough.

    25 min on the JR Sagano Line · allow 60 min · free

  • 8:30 a.m.

    Tenryu-ji temple garden

    A fourteenth-century borrowed-scenery garden that has survived eight fires. Opens at 8:30, the earliest garden opening in the city.

    4 min walk · allow 75 min · $14 for two

  • 10:15 a.m.

    Okochi Sanso Villa

    A silent-film actor's hillside garden above the grove, almost always empty. Matcha and a sweet are included in the ticket.

    10 min walk uphill · allow 75 min · $16 for two

  • 1:40 p.m.

    Kinkaku-ji, then Ryoan-ji rock garden

    The golden pavilion is a single fixed loop of forty minutes. Ryoan-ji, fifteen minutes away, is fifteen rocks in gravel and holds attention far longer.

    35 min on buses 11 and 59 · allow 165 min · $16 for two

Estimated day 3 cost $390

Lodging $168 · meals $144 · transport $32 · admissions $46.

Day 4  ·  05/25/2026

Nara day trip  ·  6.8 miles on foot

Deer, a very large Buddha, and three thousand lanterns

Forty-five minutes each way and worth a full day. Nara was the capital before Kyoto, and the scale of what survives reflects that.

  • 8:20 a.m.

    Todai-ji and the Great Buddha

    A 49-foot bronze Buddha in a hall rebuilt in 1709 at two thirds of its original width, which tells you a great deal about the original.

    45 min Kintetsu limited express · allow 105 min · $12 for two

  • 10:30 a.m.

    Nara Park and Kasuga Taisha

    Three thousand stone and bronze lanterns on the approach, and about 1,200 deer who have learned what a paper bag means. Buy crackers only from licensed vendors.

    20 min walk · allow 150 min · $10 for two

  • 1:45 p.m.

    Naramachi former merchant quarter

    Narrow lattice-fronted houses, a restored merchant residence you can walk through, and lunch away from the park crowds.

    25 min walk · allow 120 min · $52 for two

  • 5:30 p.m.

    Return to Kyoto, dinner in Pontocho

    A one-lane dining strip along the Kamo. Many rooms seat six at most and some decline walk-in visitors, so have the hotel call ahead.

    45 min train, then 15 min walk · allow 150 min · $124 for two

Estimated day 4 cost $472

Lodging $168 · meals $176 · transport $96 · admissions $22 · deer crackers $10.

Day 5  ·  05/26/2026

Northern Higashiyama  ·  5.6 miles on foot

The quiet half of the eastern hills

Same range of hills as day two, three miles north, and roughly a tenth of the people. This is the day readers most often write to us about.

  • 8:45 a.m.

    Nanzen-ji and the brick aqueduct

    A vast temple gate you can climb, and a Meiji-era Roman-style aqueduct running straight through the grounds. The subtemple garden of Konchi-in is worth the extra ticket.

    20 min subway to Keage · allow 120 min · $18 for two

  • 11:15 a.m.

    Philosopher's Path to Ginkaku-ji

    A two-mile canal walk under cherry trees, empty in late morning outside April. Ginkaku-ji at the end has the best moss garden in the city.

    Walking · allow 165 min · $8 for two

  • 2:45 p.m.

    Honen-in and a coffee stop

    A thatched gate, raked sand mounds and almost nobody. Free to enter. There is a good roaster on the lane back down toward the canal.

    10 min walk · allow 90 min · $18 for two

  • 5:00 p.m.

    Funaoka Onsen bathhouse

    A 1923 sento with the original carved woodwork intact. Learn the order of operations before you go in: wash first, then soak. Tattoo policies vary.

    30 min bus · allow 105 min · $8 for two

Estimated day 5 cost $384

Lodging $168 · meals $144 · transport $20 · admissions $52.

Day 6  ·  05/27/2026

Uji, tea and the Phoenix Hall  ·  3.4 miles on foot

A short day, on purpose

Day six of eight is where most itineraries fall apart. This one is deliberately light: one town, one temple, one long tea, and an evening with nothing scheduled.

  • 9:30 a.m.

    Byodo-in Phoenix Hall

    The building on the back of the ten-yen coin, built in 1053 and never destroyed. The museum behind it holds the original bronze phoenixes.

    30 min on the JR Nara Line · allow 120 min · $20 for two

  • 12:00 p.m.

    Uji tea street and a tasting

    Uji has grown tea since the twelfth century. A proper gyokuro tasting takes forty minutes and changes what you think green tea is.

    10 min walk · allow 120 min · $46 for two

  • 3:00 p.m.

    Return to Kyoto, Nishiki Tenmangu and Teramachi

    Covered shopping arcades with secondhand bookshops, knife makers and a shrine wedged between two storefronts. Good in rain.

    30 min train · allow 150 min · free

  • 7:00 p.m.

    Nothing scheduled

    Eat where you walked past on day two and thought about. This is the buffer for anything the week has pushed back.

    Walking · allow the evening · $80 for two

Estimated day 6 cost $356

Lodging $168 · meals $144 · transport $24 · admissions and tasting $20.

Day 7  ·  05/28/2026

Kyoto to Osaka  ·  4.8 miles on foot

Move hotels, then go loud

Osaka is twenty-nine minutes away and behaves nothing like Kyoto. The point of the two nights is to be there when the neon comes on.

  • 10:00 a.m.

    Transfer to Osaka, drop bags in Namba

    JR Special Rapid from Kyoto Station. Forward the large case by takkyubin the night before if you would rather not carry it.

    29 min train · allow 90 min · $18 for two

  • 12:30 p.m.

    Osaka Castle and the surrounding park

    A 1931 concrete reconstruction on sixteenth-century stone foundations, with a genuinely good museum inside and an observation deck on the eighth floor.

    20 min subway · allow 150 min · $12 for two

  • 3:45 p.m.

    Mercado de Kuromon, the kitchen market

    Six hundred yards of fish, fruit and grilled skewers. Later in the day than Nishiki and considerably less polished, which is the appeal.

    20 min subway · allow 90 min · $42 for two

  • 6:30 p.m.

    Dotonbori after dark

    The canal, the Glico sign, takoyaki stalls and a great deal of noise. Walk one bridge east to Hozenji Yokocho for a quieter stone-paved lane.

    10 min walk · allow 210 min · $96 for two

Estimated day 7 cost $418

Lodging $176 in Osaka · meals $166 · transport $64 · admissions $12.

Day 8  ·  05/29/2026

Osaka, then the airport  ·  2.9 miles on foot

One museum, one last bowl, one train

The flight leaves in the early evening, which leaves a real morning. Keep it near the station and keep the bags in a locker.

  • 9:00 a.m.

    Umeda Sky Building floating observatory

    Two towers joined at the fortieth floor by an open-air ring. Better in the morning than at night, and far less busy.

    15 min subway · allow 105 min · $28 for two

  • 11:30 a.m.

    Shinsekai and a kushikatsu lunch

    A 1912 neighbourhood built to look like Paris and Coney Island at once. Deep-fried skewers with one communal sauce pot, and one rule: no double dipping.

    20 min subway · allow 120 min · $54 for two

  • 2:30 p.m.

    Collect bags, Nankai Rapi:t to KIX

    Thirty-eight minutes from Namba on a train that looks like a 1950s idea of the future. Allow three hours before an international departure.

    38 min train · allow 60 min · $22 for two

Estimated day 8 cost $212

Meals $108 · transport $54 · admissions $28 · lockers $22. Departure day, no lodging.

How to adapt this

If you want Tokyo as well

Add four nights and enter through Tokyo Haneda, then take the Shinkansen west to Kyoto on day five. At that point the seven-day Japan Rail Pass becomes roughly break-even, and the fourteen-day pass does not.

If you are going in blossom season

Book lodging eight to ten months ahead and expect to pay two to three times the rates above. Move day five to first thing on day two, because the Philosopher's Path in April is worth reordering the whole trip around.

If you are traveling with children

Swap day six for the Kyoto Railway Museum, which has a working roundhouse and a steam train ride. Nara on day four already works well; deer are extremely popular with everyone under ten and mildly alarming to everyone over sixty.

If mobility is a consideration

Kiyomizu-dera, Fushimi Inari beyond the first gates and the Philosopher's Path all involve significant stairs or uneven stone. Byodo-in, Tenryu-ji, the Kyoto Railway Museum and Osaka Castle are all step-free or lift-served. We are happy to help rework a plan; write to support@meridianfieldguide.com.
