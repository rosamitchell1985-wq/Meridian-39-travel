Shorter notes from the desk

Six pieces of practical reporting kept deliberately short, because each one answers a single question.

Is TSA PreCheck worth $78 for five years?

Short answer
Yes if you fly more than twice a year from a large hub. The five-year fee works out to $15.60 a year, and the median PreCheck wait published by TSA is consistently under ten minutes. If you also travel internationally even once every two years, buy Global Entry at $120 instead, which includes PreCheck.
The catch
Enrollment appointments at some centers run six to ten weeks out. Apply before you book, not after.

How far ahead should you book a flight?

Domestic
Roughly one to three months ahead for the best median fare, and Tuesday and Wednesday departures price lower than Friday and Sunday almost universally.
International
Two to six months for Europe and Asia. Booking eleven months out rarely helps; airlines load initial inventory at high prices and adjust downward.
What actually matters more
Flexibility of dates beats timing of purchase. Shifting departure by two days saves more money more often than booking three weeks earlier.

Do you need travel insurance?

For domestic trips
Usually not. Your health insurance travels within the US, and a credit card with trip delay coverage handles most of the rest. Read the card benefit guide before buying a separate policy.
For international trips
Yes, specifically for medical and evacuation coverage. Most US health plans, including Medicare, provide little or no coverage abroad. Evacuation from a remote area can exceed $100,000.
What to look for
Medical minimum of $100,000 and evacuation minimum of $250,000. Cancellation coverage is optional and usually the most expensive part.

What does an eSIM actually cost abroad?

Typical pricing
$8 to $20 for 5GB over seven to fifteen days in Europe and most of Asia. Compare that to $10 a day for a US carrier day pass, which is $70 for a week.
Requirements
An unlocked phone from roughly 2019 onward. Install the profile on hotel wifi before you fly, and keep your home SIM active for calls and two-factor codes.

Carry-on only, or check a bag?

Carry-on wins when
Your trip is under ten days, involves more than two flights, or includes a tight connection. Lost-bag rates rise sharply with each connection.
Check a bag when
You need hiking boots, camera gear, cold-weather layers, or you are traveling with children, at which point the free hands are worth more than the wait at the belt.
The middle path
One carry-on each plus one shared checked bag, with a full change of clothes for each person split across both.

How do you avoid foreign transaction fees?

Cards
Use a credit card with no foreign transaction fee. The typical fee is 3 percent, which is $90 on a $3,000 trip for nothing.
Cash
Withdraw from a bank ATM in the local currency, never a currency exchange counter, and always decline dynamic currency conversion when the machine offers to charge you in dollars.
The one that catches people
Debit card withdrawal fees. Some US banks charge $5 plus 3 percent per withdrawal abroad. Check before you go and take out larger amounts less often.

How these guides are made

Every article carries a named author, a publication date and a last-verified date. When a fact changes, we update the article and move the verified date rather than publishing a near-identical piece with a fresh headline.

Prices and rules quoted in these guides are checked at least twice a year, and after any announced change by an airline, park service or government agency. Where a figure is an estimate rather than a published rate, we say so in the sentence rather than in a footnote.

No guide on this site is sponsored. Where an article links to a partner we earn a commission from, the link says so at the link. Our full policy is in the affiliate disclosure, and the reasoning behind it is on the about page.
