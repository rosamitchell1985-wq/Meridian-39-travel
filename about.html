Why this exists

Meridian started in 2016 as a printed zine of about forty pages, produced in a garage in Southeast Portland and mailed to roughly six hundred subscribers. It was not a business. It was a response to a particular frustration: that travel writing had become either aspirational photography with no practical information in it, or aggregated listings with no point of view.

What our subscribers actually wanted, it turned out, was the middle. They wanted somebody to have gone, to say plainly what was worth the money, and to include the opening hours. That is a boring ambition and it has turned out to be a durable one.

The software came later, and by accident. We had been publishing sample itineraries in the print issues, laid out as day plans with times and costs, and readers began writing to ask whether they could get one for a different city, or a different number of days. Answering those letters individually was not scalable. Building a tool that produced the same document was.

So the planner is not a technology company's attempt at travel content. It is a travel publication's attempt to automate the letter we were already writing by hand. That order of operations explains most of the product decisions on this site, including the ones that look commercially foolish.

The commercially foolish decisions

The free tier keeps two trips permanently rather than for fourteen days. We do not require an email address to use the planner. We do not have a newsletter popup. The packing checklist and the budget estimator on the free tools page collect nothing at all, not even an address to send a PDF to.

Each of those choices has a measurable cost, and we have measured it. We keep making them because the alternative version of this business is one where the product is the reader, and we have watched enough travel publications make that trade to know how it ends.

How the reporting works

Every stop in every destination library was written by a named editor or contributing writer who has been to it. Not researched, not aggregated from reviews. Been to.

We pay contributors a flat day rate plus expenses, and we do not accept press trips, comped hotel nights or sponsored excursions. That is unusual in travel media and it is expensive; it is also the only arrangement under which a recommendation means anything. When we cannot afford to send somebody, we do not publish the destination. That is why the index has sixteen entries and not two hundred.

Facts in the destination files are re-verified at least twice a year and after any announced change by an airline, park service or government agency. Every file carries the date it was last verified, at the top, where you can see it before you rely on it.

The masthead

Six editorial staff. Every destination file and every guide on this site carries one of these names.

Dana Okonjo Founding editor

Started the print zine in 2016 after eleven years as a features editor at a regional newspaper. Writes the planning guides and edits everything else. Based in Portland.

Curtis Boyle Contributing editor, US public lands

A former backcountry ranger who now covers the national park system, permit policy and desert safety. Wrote the parks file and the permit calendar. Based in Moab, Utah.

Tove Lindqvist Contributing editor, Nordic countries

Covers Iceland, Norway and the Faroes, and has driven the Ring Road in every month of the year, which is not a recommendation. Based in Reykjavik.

Hana Whitfield Contributing editor, Japan and Korea

Lived in Kyoto for six years and still returns three times a year. Wrote the Kyoto file, the Kansai itinerary and the first international trip guide. Based in Seattle.

Elias Moreau Editor, Latin America

Covers Mexico, Peru and Costa Rica, and maintains the Spanish-language stop libraries. Joined from a Mexico City food publication in 2021. Based in Portland.

Priya Raghunathan Head of engineering

Built the planner and maintains it. Responsible for the decision that the tool works without JavaScript for reading and with it for planning, and will explain that decision at length to anyone who asks.

How we make money, in plain English

The same list appears on the home page and the pricing page, because it should be easy to find and identical everywhere.

1. Pro subscriptions, roughly two thirds of revenue

Price
$9.00 per month or $72.00 per year, in US dollars.
What it funds
All of the editorial payroll, contributor day rates and travel expenses.

2. Affiliate commissions

From
Hotel, flight search, car rental and tour booking partners.
Your cost
Nothing. The price is identical whether you use our link or go direct.
Our rules
Labeled at the link. No editor is paid on booking volume. No partner has ever bought a placement in a destination file, and if one tried we would write about it. Full terms in the affiliate disclosure.

3. The custom itinerary service

Price
$149 domestic, $249 international, one time per trip.
What it is
A named editor rebuilds your plan and returns it within five business days.

4. Display advertising

Where
Clearly labeled slots on editorial pages only.
Where not
Never on legal pages, never on the 404 page, never above the main content of a page, and never inside a day plan.

5. White-label plans for the trade

Price
$59 to $79 per seat per month for agents and small operators.
Detail
On the for travel agents page.

What we do not do

Press trips
We do not accept them, in any form, including partial hosting.
Sponsored content
No article on this site has ever been paid for by a subject of the article.
Selling personal data
We do not sell or share personal information for cross-context behavioural advertising as defined under California law. See the do not sell or share page.
Pay-to-rank
No partner can buy a position in a destination file, an itinerary or a guide.
