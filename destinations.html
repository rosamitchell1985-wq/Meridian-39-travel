How to use this index

Three destinations have a full file: a complete overview, a budget breakdown table, a three-day sample plan, transport notes, lodging neighbourhoods and a frank safety section. Those are marked below. The remaining thirteen entries carry everything you need to decide whether a place belongs on your shortlist, and all sixteen are available inside the trip planner or as a sample itinerary.

Daily cost figures assume two travelers sharing one room at the moderate tier, and cover lodging, meals, local transport and admissions. Airfare is never included, for the reasons set out in how it works.

The full files

Three destinations reported at length, each with a budget table, a three-day plan and a safety section.

  • Iceland7 to 10 days$210 per dayDepart EWR, BOS, SEA

    Iceland

    A country the size of Kentucky with a road that circles the whole of it. The south coast delivers waterfalls, black beaches and glacier tongues within a three-hour drive of the airport, which makes it unusually forgiving for a first international road trip.

    Read the full Iceland file

  • Kyoto, Japan6 to 9 days$185 per dayDepart SFO, LAX, ORD

    Kyoto and the Kansai region

    Sixteen hundred temples, a market street, and the best rail network you will ever use. Kyoto rewards early mornings more than any city we cover, and punishes anyone who tries to see it between ten and three.

    Read the full Kyoto file

  • Utah and Arizona7 to 12 days$168 per dayDepart LAS or SLC

    The national parks road trip

    Zion, Bryce, Capitol Reef, Arches and Canyonlands in a loop of roughly 900 miles. The scenery is the easy part. The permits, the heat window and the distance between gas stations are what actually shape the trip.

    Read the full parks file

Thirteen more places on the desk

Each entry is short by design. It carries the season, the money and the honest reason to go, which is what a shortlist actually needs.

  • Banff, Alberta5 to 8 days$245 per dayDepart to YYC

    Banff and the Canadian Rockies

    Best from mid-June to late September, and quietly excellent in the second week of October when the larches turn. Private vehicles can no longer reach Moraine Lake, so shuttle reservations open in spring and vanish in hours. Budget for the parks pass, the shuttle and a car you will still need for the Icefields Parkway.

  • Lisbon, Portugal5 to 8 days$142 per dayDepart EWR, BOS, MIA

    Lisbon and Sintra

    March to May and September to October, avoiding the August heat and the cruise-ship compression of July. The cheapest western European capital we cover, with a direct overnight flight from three east coast airports. Add two days for Sintra and one for the Cascais coast, and walk downhill rather than up.

  • New Orleans, Louisiana3 to 5 days$165 per dayDomestic, MSY

    New Orleans

    February through May, or October and November. July and August are genuinely difficult, with heat indexes above 100°F by mid-morning. The best long weekend in the United States: three days covers the Quarter, the Garden District, one museum and three nights of music without hurrying.

  • New York City4 to 6 days$268 per dayDomestic, JFK, LGA, EWR

    New York City

    Late April to mid-June, and late September through early December. The most expensive destination in this index, driven almost entirely by the room rate. Stay in Brooklyn or Long Island City and the same trip costs about a third less, at a cost of fifteen minutes each way on the subway.

  • Mexico City4 to 7 days$128 per dayDepart DFW, IAH, LAX

    Mexico City

    The best value in this index and one of the great museum cities anywhere. November through April is dry season. At 7,350 feet the altitude is real, so plan a gentle first day. Three to four hour flights from most southern US hubs make it an unusually easy long weekend.

  • Tokyo and Osaka7 to 12 days$196 per dayDepart SFO, LAX, SEA, ORD

    Tokyo and Osaka

    Late March to early April for blossom, or November for maples and stable weather. Pair either city with Kyoto rather than choosing between them: the Shinkansen covers Tokyo to Kyoto in 2 hours 15 minutes and Kyoto to Osaka in under thirty.

  • Liguria, Italy5 to 7 days$178 per dayDepart to MXP or PSA

    Cinque Terre and the Ligurian coast

    May, early June or September. The five villages are connected by a local train that runs every twenty minutes, which makes a car actively unhelpful. Base yourself in Levanto or La Spezia rather than inside the villages and both the price and the crowding drop noticeably.

  • Cyclades, Greece7 to 10 days$205 per dayDepart JFK, EWR to ATH

    Santorini and the Cyclades

    Late April to mid-June, and September to mid-October. July and August are hot, expensive and full. Two islands in ten days is comfortable; four is a ferry timetable rather than a holiday. Naxos and Milos give you the same light at roughly half the room rate.

  • Peru8 to 12 days$155 per dayDepart MIA, IAH, ATL

    Cusco, the Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu

    May to September is dry season. Entry to Machu Picchu is by timed circuit ticket and sells out weeks ahead in peak months. Give yourself two full days in the Sacred Valley at 9,000 feet before going higher, because altitude sickness ruins more Peru trips than weather does.

  • Scotland7 to 10 days$162 per dayDepart EWR, BOS to EDI

    The Scottish Highlands and Skye

    May, June and September. Midges peak in July and August and they are not a joke. Driving is on the left on single-track roads with passing places, which is the real learning curve. Budget an extra ninety minutes a day over what the map suggests.

  • Dolomites, Italy6 to 9 days$188 per dayDepart to VCE or MUC

    The Dolomites

    Late June to mid-September for hiking, when the mountain huts are open and the lifts are running. The rifugio system means you can walk hut to hut with a daypack. German and Italian are both spoken; road signs carry both, which confuses the navigation more than it should.

  • Northern Thailand7 to 12 days$96 per dayDepart SFO, LAX, SEA

    Chiang Mai and the north

    November to February is cool and dry. Avoid March and April, when agricultural burning pushes air quality into genuinely unhealthy territory. The cheapest destination in this index by a wide margin, and the one where a comfortable-tier budget buys the largest jump in quality.

  • Nepal12 to 18 days$88 per dayDepart via DOH or IST

    Kathmandu and the Annapurna foothills

    October and November, then March to early May. The longest trip in this index and the only one where the flight itself takes the better part of two days. A teahouse trek needs no camping equipment, a guide is now mandatory in the national parks, and altitude planning is not optional.
